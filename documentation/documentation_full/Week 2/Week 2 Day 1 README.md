# Quest Log - Day 1 Detailed Documentation

Today was the day we started our full project called the **Quest Log** and received our final teams. My new team consisted of someone I already knew and two other people I had never met before. We introduced ourselves and talked about our strengths and weaknesses. We also discussed how we wanted to approach the Quest Log and how we could work together during the project.

---

The first week of the Quest Log appeared to be quite doable because we had already worked with similar programming concepts, including **wall following** and **line following**, during the prerequisite course. However, after looking ahead, we also saw more advanced topics such as **sign detection through machine learning** and **SLAM, or simultaneous localization and mapping**. These topics are used in real autonomous vehicles and ongoing prototypes, so they will require us to learn new programming and robotics concepts during the project.

---

To begin our work, we created **shared drives and shared folders** so that the team could organize notes, code, documentation, and other project materials. We also created a **group GitHub repository** so that we could share our code and keep track of changes throughout the Quest Log.

---

Our first lab was the **line follower**. The initial plan was to prepare the line-following program, connect it to the RACECAR, and test it on the physical course. During testing, we would observe whether the car detected and followed the line correctly. We would then adjust the program based on the car's behavior and record our changes and results.

---

Day 1 was mainly used for **team formation, project organization, and preparation for the first lab**. We did not complete a physical RACECAR test, so we did not collect performance results or testing data. Our next step is to begin implementing the line follower, review how each part of the code works, and test the first version on the RACECAR.

---

The Quest Log will require us to balance **programming, hardware testing, documentation, and lecture assignments**. As the project continues, we expect the work to become more challenging, especially when we begin sign detection and SLAM. Keeping the shared folders and GitHub repository organized will help the team coordinate its work and document each test.
